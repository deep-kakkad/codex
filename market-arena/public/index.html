How it works

From two ideas to a verdict in three steps

Step 1

Describe the market

Start from a ready-made case or write your own: who the buyers are, what they care about, what they doubt. Four to sixteen buyers, grouped into segments.

Step 2

Write the versions

A headline, a value proposition and a price for each, plus a call to action, an offer or proof if you want to test those too.

Step 3

Run it

Every buyer sees every version side by side and picks one, or walks away. You get the verdict, who chose what, and why the rest said no.

Evidence, not a score

Every number opens its evidence

A figure in the report is a claim. Select it and you see the buyers behind it: who chose what, how sure they were, and the objection that held the others back. Then change one thing and run it again.

  • The verdictanswered in the terms of the question you asked
  • The funnelwhere each version lost people, from noticed to bought

  • The next testthe one change worth making before you run again

Method

Honest about what it is

A simulator, not a crystal ball. It is for deciding which idea deserves a real test, and it is built so you can see how far to trust each result.

12.5 pts

Order doesn't win

Listing a version first moved its share by 12.5 points in our tests. So every buyer sees the versions in a different order, and the effect cancels out.

~1 pt

Ties are called ties

Identical re-runs move share by about a point. Gaps under five points are reported as a tie, not a win.

5

Questions, no quotes

Each buyer answers typed questions: would they notice it, how appealing it is, do they believe it, what holds them back, and which they'd buy. Nothing is written in a buyer's voice.

Pinned

Results don't drift

The buyer model's version is fixed, so a saved round means the same thing next month as it did today.
