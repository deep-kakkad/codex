How it works

Three steps. No survey. No waiting.

Step 1

Pick your buyers

Start from a ready-made case, write your own, or build them from your customer reviews and interview notes. 4 to 16 buyers, grouped the way your market is.

Step 2

Write your versions

A headline, a promise and a price for each. Add a button, an offer or a proof point if you want to test those too. Or paste a web page, yours or a rival's, and we'll fill it in.

Step 3

Run the round

Every buyer sees every ad side by side and picks one, or walks away. You get the winner, who picked what, and why the rest said no.

The part other tests skip

It tells you which line did the work.

Knowing an ad won is nice. Knowing why is money. Every buyer names the part that sold them and the part that put them off, and we mark both right on the ad.

To check it works, we hid a bad line in one of our own test ads. The buyers found it.

The fine print, in large print

A simulator, not a crystal ball

Use it to decide which idea deserves real money and a real test. Here's how we keep it honest, so you know how far to trust each result.

12.5 pts

Going first isn't a prize

In our tests, showing a version first bumped it 12.5 points. So every buyer sees the ads in a different order, and the bump cancels out.

~1 pt

A tie is a tie

Run the same round twice and the numbers move about a point. Anything under 5 points apart, we call a tie. Not a win.

0

Words put in a buyer's mouth

Buyers answer multiple-choice questions: did they notice it, do they want it, do they believe it, what's stopping them, which would they buy. We never write a quote and pretend a buyer said it.

Pinned

Same round, same meaning

The buyer model's version is locked. A round you save today means the same thing next month.
